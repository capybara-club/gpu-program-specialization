# Heavy multi-RHS system generation and scoring

## Result

The scratch generator can now produce a complete candidate system with an independently configured AST space for every generated RHS. The implementation is C99, allocation-free during iteration, deterministic by seed, and can generate exact-depth trees even when the combinatorial search-space count exceeds 64 bits.

On Rohini's RTX 5090, four generated depth-4 RHS expressions sustained **251,041 complete systems/s**, **1.004 million generated RHS ASTs/s**, and **64.27 million trajectory configurations/s** through the complete specialization/load/run/unload pipeline. Increasing from one generated RHS to four reduced complete-system configuration throughput by only 6.8%. Specialized register use remained 42.

## Workload

- Four-state RK4 system.
- 4,096 candidate systems.
- One to four generated RHS expressions; earlier derivatives remain fixed when fewer than four are generated.
- Three trajectories, eight observations, and two RK4 substeps per observation.
- Five two-way toggle bits and eight constant banks: 256 configurations/system.
- Exact-depth structural-random trees from state leaves, four constant slots, literals `0`, `1`, `-1`, and `2`, and `add`, `sub`, `mul`, and `neg`.
- 64 systems/module, 512 SASS instructions/system, one specialization worker, two CUBIN slots, two loaded modules, and two streams.
- Timed scope includes C99 specialization, eager load, lookup, execution, retirement, and unload. It excludes corpus construction, allocation, upload, download, and CPU replay.

Structural-random sampling is not uniform over all syntactic expressions and does not guarantee uniqueness. It chooses tree structure directly so heavy spaces are usable after ranked enumeration overflows 64 bits. This is an explicit different sampling regime, not a replacement for uniform ranked random selection when the exact space is countable.

## Generated-RHS scaling at exact depth 4

All systems execute four derivatives. The generated/fixed split changes from one generated plus three simple fixed RHS expressions to four generated expressions.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 40.78 | 18.22 | 42 | 269,485 | 269,485 | 68.99M |
| 2 | 2 | 40.52 | 35.39 | 42 | 268,218 | 536,436 | 68.66M |
| 3 | 1 | 40.59 | 53.16 | 42 | 266,560 | 799,680 | 68.24M |
| 4 | 0 | 40.50 | 70.34 | 42 | 251,041 | 1,004,165 | 64.27M |

The weak decline through three generated RHS expressions shows that module handling and the common integration scaffold still account for substantial time. The fourth generated RHS makes execution measurably heavier, but it does not create a register-use cliff.

## Four-generated-RHS depth scaling

| Exact depth | Mean bytes/RHS | Mean body instructions/system | Maximum body instructions/system | Registers | Systems/s | Generated RHS AST/s | Configurations/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 18.71 | 32.97 | 46 | 39 | 283,445 | 1,133,781 | 72.56M |
| 4 | 40.50 | 70.34 | 117 | 42 | 251,041 | 1,004,165 | 64.27M |
| 6 | 77.22 | 133.86 | 183 | 45 | 219,397 | 877,588 | 56.17M |
| 8 | 135.82 | 241.66 | 356 | 46 | 175,041 | 700,165 | 44.81M |

Depth 8 still specialized and executed with zero specialization or CUDA failures. Register use increased gradually from 39 to 46; expression size primarily costs instructions and execution time, not persistent registers. The 512-instruction per-system arena remained sufficient for every sampled system, with a measured maximum body of 356 instructions.

## Correctness and numerical boundary

Depths 2, 4, and 6 each passed 64 independent complete-trajectory CPU/GPU replays. Their maximum relative MSE differences were between `5.8e-7` and `1.23e-6`.

The full depth-8 sweep encountered one finite explosive trajectory at system 585/configuration 159 whose CPU and GPU MSE differed by 0.38%. The programs used no approximate transcendental or division instruction; ordinary FP32 differences were amplified by the rollout. The throughput result is therefore valid, but its full-corpus CPU numerical-equivalence coverage is conditional. A focused 64-system depth-8 run passed all 64 replays, with four mutually invalid CPU/GPU scores and a maximum finite relative difference of `2.00e-6`.

This is a scoring-policy issue for unstable systems, not evidence of an AST dispatch failure. A future numerical policy should distinguish stable agreement, mutually invalid trajectories, and sensitivity-amplified finite disagreement rather than silently broadening one global tolerance.

## Compilation

The first 64-system/512-instruction template creation took 9.58 seconds. Subsequent identical creations used the compiler cache and took 34-44 ms. Those warm times are not cold compilation measurements.

## Interpretation

The current shape supports substantially heavier systems than the earlier one-candidate-RHS benchmark without a register-pressure collapse. The practical limit encountered here is trajectory numerical sensitivity; the sampled depth-8 programs did not hit specialization capacity. The next useful implementation step is to expose independent per-RHS generator constraints through the higher-level problem description rather than increasing the scratch maximum depth further.
